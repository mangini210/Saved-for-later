# DNA from old skeleton suggests humanity’s been here longer than we thought

**URL:** https://arstechnica.com/science/2017/10/dna-from-old-skeleton-suggests-humanitys-been-here-longer-than-we-thought/  
**Date:** 15 May 2026  
**Summary type:** Long Summary

---

## Abstract
Genomic analysis of a South African Stone Age skeleton suggests that the split between modern human lineages occurred between 260,000 and 350,000 years ago, significantly pushing back the estimated timeline for the origin of *Homo sapiens*.

## Introduction
Determining the exact dawn of humanity has long been complicated by fragmentary fossil records and the presence of archaic human relatives like Neanderthals. While previous genetic and paleontological estimates often placed the emergence of modern humans around 150,000 years ago, new genomic evidence from ancient remains is challenging these established timelines. This research underscores Africa's central role in evolution and demonstrates how ancient DNA can clarify lineage splits that modern genetic diversity might otherwise obscure.

## Main Points
*   The Khoe-San people of southern Africa possess the highest genetic diversity of any extant human group, marking them as the earliest branching lineage of modern humanity.
*   Previous age estimates for this lineage were likely underestimated because modern Khoe-San DNA has been influenced by later migrations, including the Bantu expansion and influxes from East Africa.
*   By sequencing the genome of a Stone Age hunter-gatherer skeleton that predates these migrations, researchers identified a "purer" ancestral lineage unaffected by later interbreeding.
*   The new data suggests the human evolutionary split happened between 285,000 and 365,000 years ago, roughly doubling earlier estimates.
*   The study also sequenced Iron Age skeletons, finding that the Bantu expansion brought genetic variants for disease resistance—such as malaria and sleeping sickness—that were absent in Stone Age populations.
*   The revised timeline aligns with the technological transition to the Middle Stone Age and recent discoveries of semi-modern fossils in other parts of Africa.

## Conclusions
The research provides compelling evidence that modern humans have existed for significantly longer than the previously accepted 200,000-year threshold. While researchers admit that variables like mutation rates and potential interbreeding with archaic humans could influence these dates, the findings suggest a strong correlation between the biological emergence of humans and a major "mental leap" in tool technology. Ultimately, the study reinforces the idea that the secret to understanding the deep history of our species lies in the genetic diversity found within Africa.

## Interesting Facts & Data
*   The most extreme genetic split identified in the study was between the Khoe-San and the Mandinka people of West Africa, dated to approximately 356,000 years ago.
*   A date of 300,000 years ago places the origin of modern humans roughly halfway between the present day and the point when Neanderthals and Denisovans diverged from our lineage.
*   Modern Khoe-San genomes were found to contain DNA from East African populations that had already interbred with Eurasians before moving south.
*   The researchers were only able to extract one high-quality genome from the three Stone Age bones sampled for the study.
